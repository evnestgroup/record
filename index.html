Is the edge real? Two stricter tests

Won vs. fair odds compares wins with what Pinnacle's no-vig price said each pick's chance was. A real edge wins at least that often; luck can't hide for long here. Beat the close only counts closing prices we actually re-checked before the game. Until Nov 17 many weren't, and they just repeated the price at posting, which made our old "beat the close" number look better than it was.

What this shows so far: moneylines have won about as often as the odds said, and they've made money. Props haven't. They beat Pinnacle's closing price most of the time, but Pinnacle's prop prices often don't move at all, and props have won less often than its odds said. Since Nov 26, props also have to beat the betting exchanges' price before they're posted.

How to check it yourself

Each pick's fingerprint is the SHA-256 of the text shown when you open it. That text starts with the previous pick's fingerprint, so changing, removing or back-dating any pick breaks every fingerprint after it. Open a pick to see its text, or download record.json and hash it with any tool.

Sealed picks are for games that haven't started. Only their fingerprint is shown until kickoff. It's published before the game, so it couldn't have been made after.

Results and closing line value are added after each game and aren't part of the fingerprint. They come from the final score and Pinnacle's last price before the game. A "–" in the CLV column means that pick's closing price wasn't re-checked before the game, so it isn't counted.
